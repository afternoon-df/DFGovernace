# Taiwan Deepfake Law Summary

## Core Legal Framework
- Taiwan combines two in-force statutory anchors: Presidential and Vice Presidential Election and Recall Act Article 47-3, added on 9 June 2023, and Criminal Code Chapter 28-1 as amended through 1 August 2025. (Ref1, Ref2)
- Article 47-3 defines deepfakes for the election procedure and creates investigation, notice, restriction/removal, and evidence-retention mechanisms. Criminal Code Article 319-4 directly covers harmful synthetic sexual images generated by computer synthesis or other technological methods. (Ref1, Ref2)

## Summary
- Taiwan has a functionally split model: election integrity is governed through an administrative/procedural deepfake response, while synthetic sexual-image harms are addressed through criminal law. (Ref1, Ref2)
- Election Article 47-3 permits investigation requests during the election period and, after notice, requires broadcasters or internet platforms to restrict dissemination, remove content, and retain relevant data. (Ref1)
- Criminal Code Article 319-4 provides a base maximum of five years and NT$500,000, with profit-related aggravation up to seven years and NT$700,000. (Ref2)

## Policy Dimensions
- `Labeling status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. The reviewed sources use investigation, restriction/removal, and criminalization rather than a general synthetic-content label mandate. (Ref1, Ref2) Relevant text: Election Act Art. 47-3; Criminal Code Art. 319-4.
- `Election-related status`: legislated. Article 47-3 defines deepfakes and provides an election-period investigation and response mechanism. (Ref1) Relevant text: p. 1, Art. 47-3 paras. 1-2.
- `Non-consensual deepfake status`: legislated. Criminal Code Article 319-4 directly covers harmful synthetic sexual images produced by computer synthesis or other technological methods. (Ref2) Relevant text: pp. 99-101, especially p. 101, Art. 319-4.
- `Service-provider/platform liability status`: legislated for elections. After statutory notice, broadcasters and internet platforms may be required to restrict or remove dissemination and retain evidence. (Ref1) Relevant text: pp. 1-2, Art. 47-3 paras. 3-5.
- `Penalty/sanctions codification status`: legislated. Article 319-4 provides up to five years and NT$500,000, aggravated for profit up to seven years and NT$700,000. (Ref2) Relevant text: p. 101, Art. 319-4.

Ref1: `sources/official/taiwan/1_official-law-act/taiwan-2023-presidential-vice-presidential-election-recall-act-article-47-3-en.pdf`
Ref2: `sources/official/taiwan/1_official-law-act/taiwan-2025-criminal-code-republic-of-china-en.pdf`

## Practice Notes (by Dimension)
- `Labeling`: do not recast restriction/removal procedures as a universal label requirement.
- `Election`: keep the Article 47-3 mechanism scoped to election-period procedures.
- `Non-consensual deepfakes`: cite the computer-synthesis wording in Article 319-4.
- `Service-provider/platform liability`: platform duties are strongest after statutory notice and within the election context.
- `Penalty`: preserve the distinction between the base offence and profit-related aggravated range.

## Verify Before Use
- Confirm later amendments to both statutes before relying on the stated current text dates.
- Keep election and sexual-image mechanisms as distinct legal pathways.
