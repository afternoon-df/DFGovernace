# Singapore Deepfake Law Summary

## Core Legal Framework
- The `Elections (Integrity of Online Advertising) (Amendment) Act 2024`, Act No. 34 of 2024, is the principal binding deepfake-related law in the reviewed Singapore corpus. It amends both the Parliamentary Elections Act 1954 and the Presidential Elections Act 1991 and has been in force since 22 January 2025. (Ref1, Ref4)
- `Bill No. 29/2024` is the historical proposal version of the same legislative measure. It is retained for legislative history but is not counted as a separate law. (Ref2)
- The court-user generative AI guide is non-binding official guidance. It addresses verification and responsibility in court-related AI use, but it is not a deepfake offence or platform-liability statute. (Ref3)

## Summary
- Singapore's strongest direct deepfake control is election-specific. During an election period, the Act prohibits online election advertising that contains a realistic but false representation of a candidate generated or manipulated by digital means; generative AI is expressly identified as an example of such a digital means. (Ref1)
- The regime combines a publication offence with an official-direction mechanism. A candidate may request action by the Returning Officer, and directions may be issued to relevant persons, including social media service providers. (Ref1)
- This is not a general-purpose deepfake code. The reviewed Act does not create a general disclosure-label duty or a dedicated non-consensual intimate-deepfake offence outside the election context. (Ref1)
- Dimensionally, Singapore is strong for election integrity, election-specific provider enforcement, and codified sanctions, but has no dedicated rule in this corpus for general labeling or non-consensual deepfakes. (Ref1, Ref4)

## Policy Dimensions
- `Labeling status`: No Law. The reviewed Act prohibits specified false candidate representations and authorizes directions, but it does not impose a general statutory label or disclosure requirement for synthetic media. Do not treat the prohibition itself as a labeling mandate. (Ref1) See Act PDF pp. 2-4, inserted Parliamentary Elections Act section 61MA, and pp. 6-8, inserted Presidential Elections Act section 42LA.
- `Election-related status`: Strong. In force since 22 January 2025, the Act directly criminalizes qualifying online election advertising containing realistic but false digitally generated or manipulated candidate representations during election periods. (Ref1, Ref4) See Act PDF pp. 2-4, section 61MA, and pp. 6-8, section 42LA; Commencement Notification S 47/2025, paragraph 2.
- `Non-consensual deepfake status`: No Law. The reviewed official corpus does not establish a dedicated non-consensual intimate-deepfake offence. Election-candidate misrepresentation should not be generalized into that separate harm category. (Ref1)
- `Service-provider/platform liability status`: Strong. The in-force direction regime reaches social media service providers, but only within the election-law scope reviewed here. A provider that, without reasonable excuse, fails to comply with a direction may be fined up to SGD 1,000,000. (Ref1, Ref4) See Act PDF pp. 4-5, amended section 61N(2), and p. 8, amended section 42M(2).
- `Penalty/sanctions codification status`: Strong. Publishing the prohibited election advertising is punishable by a fine up to SGD 1,000, imprisonment up to 12 months, or both. Direction non-compliance by a social media service provider carries a fine up to SGD 1,000,000; other relevant persons face up to SGD 1,000, imprisonment up to 12 months, or both. (Ref1, Ref4) See section 61MA(2), section 42LA(2), amended section 61N(2), and amended section 42M(2).

## Practice Notes (by Dimension)
- `Labeling`: do not state that Singapore has a general deepfake-labeling law on the basis of this Act. Any voluntary label or provenance control should be described as an operational safeguard, not the statutory mechanism evidenced here.
- `Election`: maintain a dedicated election-period review path for realistic candidate depictions, with rapid escalation to preserve evidence and respond to Returning Officer directions.
- `Non-consensual deepfakes`: use separate internal abuse and victim-safety controls because this election statute does not supply a dedicated intimate-deepfake offence.
- `Service-provider/platform liability`: maintain a documented notice-direction-action chain, named escalation owners, and records capable of demonstrating timely compliance.
- `Penalty`: separate publisher liability, social media provider direction non-compliance, and other relevant-person non-compliance in sanctions matrices.

## Verify Before Use
- The Act is election-specific; do not apply its offences or platform duties as a general all-domain deepfake regime.
- Ref2 is legislative history, Ref3 is guidance, and Ref4 establishes commencement. Only Ref1 supplies the substantive offence and penalty clauses summarized above.
