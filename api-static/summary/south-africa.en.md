# South Africa Deepfake Law Summary

## Core Legal Framework
- The Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 is in force for the reviewed provisions from 1 December 2021. It is not a general deepfake statute, but s. 16 expressly includes intimate depictions that are real or simulated and made by any means. (Ref1)
- Sections 20-22 support protective and removal orders, including directions to electronic communications service providers to remove or disable access. (Ref1)

## Summary
- South Africa provides direct in-force coverage for non-consensual simulated intimate images. Section 16 prohibits intentional disclosure without consent and does not require the depiction to be a real recording. (Ref1)
- Section 19(7) provides a fine or imprisonment up to three years, or both, for offences including s. 16. (Ref1)
- Provider responsibility is order-based: the Act enables courts to direct removal or access disabling. It should not be generalized into a proactive monitoring duty. (Ref1)

## Policy Dimensions
- `Labeling status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. The Cybercrimes Act does not establish a general synthetic-media label or watermark mandate. (Ref1) Relevant text: reviewed ss. 16 and 19-22.
- `Election-related status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. No election-specific deepfake rule was identified. (Ref1) Relevant text: reviewed Act.
- `Non-consensual deepfake status`: legislated. Section 16 expressly covers an intimate depiction that is real or simulated and made by any means. (Ref1) Relevant text: p. 22, s. 16.
- `Service-provider/platform liability status`: legislated but order-based. Courts may direct electronic communications service providers to remove or disable access under the protective-order framework. (Ref1) Relevant text: pp. 28-36, ss. 20-22.
- `Penalty/sanctions codification status`: legislated. Section 19(7) provides a fine, imprisonment up to three years, or both for s. 16. (Ref1) Relevant text: p. 28, s. 19(7).

Ref1: `sources/official/south-africa/1_official-law-act/south-africa-2020-cybercrimes-act-no-19-en-af.pdf`

## Practice Notes (by Dimension)
- `Labeling`: do not infer content labeling from the Act's criminal and remedial provisions.
- `Election`: election-specific regulation remains unconfirmed in this corpus.
- `Non-consensual deepfakes`: the words “real or simulated” are the direct synthetic-media anchor.
- `Service-provider/platform liability`: describe the duty as compliance with a court order, not proactive monitoring.
- `Penalty`: link the three-year maximum specifically to s. 19(7) and s. 16.

## Verify Before Use
- Check later amendments or election statutes before treating this source as exhaustive.
- Keep the direct intimate-image scope separate from unrelated deepfake harms.
