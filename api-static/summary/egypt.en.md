# Egypt Deepfake Law Summary

## Core Legal Framework
- Egypt is represented by in-force Anti-Cyber and Information Technology Crimes Law No. 175 of 2018. The official Gazette PDF required Arabic OCR, so the page images remain controlling. (Ref1)
- The reviewed provisions provide adjacent routes for false attribution, impersonation, privacy violations, unlawful information content, and general service-provider duties; they do not expressly establish a dedicated deepfake regime. (Ref1)

## Summary
- Apparent Article 24 addresses creation of an email, website, or private account falsely attributed to another person and increases punishment where it is used to cause harm. Apparent Article 25 addresses privacy and unlawful-content conduct. (Ref1)
- General provider data duties and sanctions appear elsewhere in the Act, but they must not be presented as deepfake-specific platform obligations. (Ref1)
- Article numbering is OCR-sensitive. Any quotation or exact penalty statement requires visual confirmation against the official Gazette pages. (Ref1)

## Policy Dimensions
- `Labeling status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. No synthetic-media disclosure, watermarking, or labeling mandate was identified. (Ref1) Relevant text: official Gazette PDF, reviewed pp. 5-20.
- `Election-related status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. No election-specific deepfake rule was identified. (Ref1) Relevant text: official Gazette PDF, reviewed provisions.
- `Non-consensual deepfake status`: not clearly deepfake-specific. Apparent Arts. 24-25 provide adjacent false-attribution and privacy pathways, but no express synthetic-media term was verified. (Ref1) Relevant text: OCR p. 16 and pp. 16-17; article numerals require visual confirmation.
- `Service-provider/platform liability status`: partly applicable general duties. The Act contains provider data and compliance obligations, but not a dedicated deepfake notice-and-removal regime. (Ref1) Relevant text: OCR pp. 5-7 and pp. 18-20.
- `Penalty/sanctions codification status`: not clearly deepfake-specific. General cybercrime sanctions are in force, but exact article numbers and penalty figures remain verification-sensitive in the OCR text. (Ref1) Relevant text: OCR pp. 16-20; verify against page images.

Ref1: `sources/official/egypt/1_official-law-act/egypt-2018-anti-cyber-information-technology-crimes-law-no-175-ar.pdf`

## Practice Notes (by Dimension)
- `Labeling`: do not infer disclosure duties from general cybercrime provisions.
- `Election`: keep election-specific coverage unconfirmed.
- `Non-consensual deepfakes`: use false-attribution and privacy provisions only as adjacent legal pathways.
- `Service-provider/platform liability`: distinguish general data/cooperation duties from content-specific takedown rules.
- `Penalty`: visually verify the Arabic Gazette before quoting an article number or amount.

## Verify Before Use
- Confirm apparent Arts. 24-25 and their penalties from the official page images or a verified official text layer.
- Do not describe Law No. 175/2018 as a dedicated deepfake statute.
