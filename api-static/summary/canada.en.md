# Canada Deepfake Law Summary

## Core Legal Framework
- The current corpus contains Bill C-16, the Protecting Victims Act, introduced on 9 December 2025. It is an official federal bill and must not be described as enacted or in force. (Ref1)
- The bill would expand Criminal Code intimate-image protections to electronic visual representations that are likely to be mistaken for recordings of an identifiable person. (Ref1)

## Summary
- Canada's current direct synthetic-media anchor is proposal-stage and focused on non-consensual intimate imagery rather than a general deepfake regime. (Ref1)
- Proposed Criminal Code s. 162.1 would cover both publication and threats to publish qualifying synthetic intimate representations. The proposed indictable maximum is ten years, with a summary-conviction route also available. (Ref1)
- The reviewed bill does not establish a general labeling mandate, election-specific rule, or dedicated platform-duty framework. (Ref1)

## Policy Dimensions
- `Labeling status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. Bill C-16 does not create a general deepfake disclosure, watermarking, or labeling mandate. (Ref1) Relevant text: p. 15, proposed Criminal Code s. 162.1.
- `Election-related status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. The reviewed bill is directed to intimate-image harms, not election integrity. (Ref1) Relevant text: p. 1 legislative summary and p. 15 proposed s. 162.1.
- `Non-consensual deepfake status`: proposed coverage. The bill would expressly extend intimate-image rules to synthetic visual representations likely to be mistaken for authentic recordings. (Ref1) Relevant text: p. 15, proposed s. 162.1(1.1)-(2).
- `Service-provider/platform liability status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. No deepfake-specific notice, removal, or retention duty was identified in Bill C-16. (Ref1) Relevant text: reviewed bill scope.
- `Penalty/sanctions codification status`: proposed criminal penalties. The indictable route would carry imprisonment of up to ten years, with an alternative summary-conviction route. (Ref1) Relevant text: p. 15, proposed s. 162.1.

Ref1: `sources/official/canada/2_draft/canada-2025-bill-c-16-protecting-victims-act-en-fr.pdf`

## Practice Notes (by Dimension)
- `Labeling`: do not infer a general Canadian synthetic-media label rule from Bill C-16.
- `Election`: keep election regulation unconfirmed in this corpus.
- `Non-consensual deepfakes`: distinguish the bill's direct synthetic-intimate-image wording from enacted law; the proposal stage controls the tag.
- `Service-provider/platform liability`: do not convert publication offences into a proactive platform-duty regime.
- `Penalty`: present the ten-year maximum as proposed, not in force.
