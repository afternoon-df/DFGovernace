# Russia Deepfake Law Summary

## Core Legal Framework
- The current Russia corpus contains one official statutory source: Rospatent's English consolidated edition of Parts One to Four of the Civil Code of the Russian Federation, current to 21 April 2026. The underlying Parts were enacted between 1994 and 2006; the 2026 date identifies this consolidated edition, not a new deepfake act. (Ref1)
- Russia is therefore represented here by an adjacent civil-rights framework, not a dedicated deepfake or synthetic-media regime. The strongest relevant anchors protect non-material interests, reputation, a citizen's depiction, and private-life information. (Ref1)

## Summary
- Article 152.1 generally requires consent for publication and further use of a citizen's depiction, subject to public-interest, public-event, and paid-sitting exceptions. It also permits withdrawal or destruction of infringing media and deletion or cessation of further Internet dissemination. (Ref1)
- Articles 150-152.2 add protection for dignity, reputation, privacy, and private-life information. Article 152 permits correction, deletion, cessation of dissemination, loss recovery, and moral-harm compensation; Article 152.2 prohibits non-consensual collection, storage, dissemination, and use of private-life information subject to stated exceptions. (Ref1)
- These provisions may support a civil claim when manipulated media uses a person's depiction or private-life information, but the reviewed text contains no express reference to deepfakes, synthetic media, generative AI, or digital replicas. Application to a particular deepfake would therefore require a fact-specific legal interpretation. (Ref1)
- The current source does not establish a general synthetic-media label, an election-deepfake rule, a platform-specific notice-and-removal duty, or a dedicated deepfake criminal or administrative sanction. (Ref1)

## Policy Dimensions
- `Labeling status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. The Civil Code provisions reviewed here protect personal interests and provide civil remedies, but do not require disclosure, watermarking, or labeling of AI-generated or manipulated media. (Ref1) Relevant text: no labeling provision identified; compare Articles 150-152.2, PDF pp. 65-66, normalized lines 2200-2259.
- `Election-related status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. No election, campaign, or voting-specific synthetic-media rule was identified in the reviewed Civil Code source. (Ref1) Relevant text: no election-specific provision identified in Ref1.
- `Non-consensual deepfake status`: weak/adjacent. Article 152.1(1) requires consent for publication and further use of a citizen's depiction, Article 152.1(3) supports Internet deletion and cessation, and Article 152.2 addresses non-consensual private-life information. None expressly defines or prohibits deepfakes or non-consensual intimate synthetic media. (Ref1) Relevant text: Article 152.1(1)-(3) and Article 152.2(1), (3)-(4), PDF p. 66, normalized lines 2236-2256.
- `Service-provider/platform liability status`: no dedicated law in the current corpus. The Internet deletion remedy in Article 152.1(3) is a right held by the affected citizen; the reviewed text does not itself impose a deepfake-specific monitoring, labeling, notice-handling, or takedown system on platforms. (Ref1) Relevant text: Article 152.1(3), PDF p. 66, normalized lines 2245-2246.
- `Penalty/sanctions codification status`: weak/adjacent. Articles 12, 15, 151, and 152 provide civil remedies including restoration, suppression, losses, and moral-harm compensation, but the reviewed source does not specify a deepfake offense, fixed fine, or imprisonment term. (Ref1) Relevant text: Articles 12 and 15, PDF p. 4, normalized lines 143-170; Articles 151 and 152(9), PDF pp. 65-66, normalized lines 2208-2233.

Ref1: `sources/official/russia/1_official-law-act/russia-2026-civil-code-russian-federation-parts-one-four-en.pdf`

## Practice Notes (by Dimension)
- `Labeling`: do not infer a synthetic-media disclosure duty from general civil-rights protection.
- `Election`: treat election-deepfake regulation as unconfirmed until an official election-law source is added.
- `Non-consensual deepfakes`: test whether the disputed output constitutes a use of the claimant's depiction, false information, or private-life information; do not describe Article 152.1 as a dedicated NCII deepfake offense.
- `Service-provider/platform liability`: distinguish a claimant's deletion or cessation remedy from a statutory platform compliance system.
- `Penalty`: describe the available consequences as civil remedies; do not convert open-ended loss or moral-harm compensation into a normalized criminal fine.

## Verify Before Use
- This summary relies on the official Rospatent English consolidation. It does not establish that every possible deepfake claim would satisfy the Civil Code's elements or defeat its exceptions.
- Add official Russian election, criminal, administrative, communications, or platform legislation before changing the corresponding no-law or weak/adjacent statuses.
