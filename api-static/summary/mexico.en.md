# Mexico Deepfake Law Summary

## Core Legal Framework
- Mexico has two distinct tracks in the current corpus. First, the Federal Criminal Code Article 199 Octies proposal targets AI-created intimate sexual content, but remains proposal-stage here. (Ref1)
- Second, the 14 May 2026 DOF labor/copyright reform and the current LFDA text create an in-force performer image/voice AI-rights pathway: express consent, contract conditions, remuneration, AI-generated results, clones, identifiable voice simulation, and prior written agreement. (Ref2, Ref3)

## Summary
- Mexico is no longer only a proposed-criminal-law case. Its enacted performer-rights reform is highly relevant to synthetic-media production involving performers' image, voice, characters, AI cloning, and market substitution. (Ref2, Ref3)
- The enacted track is scope-limited: it protects performers/interpreters/executants and related copyright/neighboring-rights contexts. It should not be overstated as a general deepfake statute for all persons or all platforms. (Ref2, Ref3)
- The criminal intimate-content route remains important but must stay coded as proposed until enacted Federal Criminal Code text is added. (Ref1)

## Policy Dimensions
- `Labeling status`: no dedicated law. Current Mexico files do not establish a general public deepfake-labeling or watermarking mandate. The in-force rules are consent/contract/remuneration controls, not public disclosure labels. (Ref2, Ref3) Relevant text: Article 305 Bis and LFDA Article 87.
- `Election-related status`: no dedicated law. Current Mexico files do not identify election-specific synthetic-media rules. (Ref1, Ref2, Ref3) Relevant text: no election-specific article identified in the reviewed files.
- `Non-consensual deepfake status`: partly legislated/strong for performer image and voice, proposed for intimate AI content. In-force LFDA provisions require express consent for image/voice use, extend protection to AI-generated results, and require prior written agreement for AI cloning or supplantation of voice/image. The intimate-content criminal rule remains proposed. (Ref1, Ref2, Ref3) Relevant text: Article 87, Article 118(VII), Article 121; proposed Article 199 Octies.
- `Service-provider/platform liability status`: weak/limited. LFDA includes copyright/related-rights notice-removal or disable-access context for hosted material without consent, but the current corpus does not establish a dedicated deepfake platform-duty regime. (Ref3) Relevant text: LFDA online removal context around normalized line 532.
- `Penalty/sanctions codification status`: mixed. Performer image/voice AI rights are in-force as rights/remedy controls, while the AI intimate-content criminal penalty route is still proposed and article-level penalty values are pending. (Ref1, Ref2, Ref3) Relevant text: proposed Article 199 Octies; LFDA Articles 87, 118(VII), 121.

## Practice Notes (by Dimension)
- `Labeling`: do not treat contract consent language as a public synthetic-media label mandate.
- `Election`: keep election deepfake claims under review until an official election-law source is added.
- `Non-consensual deepfakes`: separate enacted performer image/voice consent rights from the proposed criminal intimate-content bill.
- `Service-provider/platform liability`: use LFDA notice-removal language only for copyright/related-rights hosting contexts.
- `Penalty`: mark intimate-content criminal penalties as proposed; use enacted LFDA provisions for rights/remedies, consent, authorization, and remuneration analysis.
